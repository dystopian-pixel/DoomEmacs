* General

** VSCode-like

This subsection eases the mental switch for folks coming from popular mainstream editors. It mirrors familiar patterns so your hands can do what they already know. The goal is to reduce surprise and cut the learning curve. You get a comfortable runway while still benefiting from the rest of the configuration.

#+begin_src emacs-lisp
;;; keybindings.el --- Custom keybindings -*- lexical-binding: t; -*-

;; VSCode-like bindings in visual, normal, and insert modes
(map! :v "C-c" #'kill-ring-save
      :v "C-x" #'kill-region
      :v "C-v" #'yank
      :v "C-a" #'mark-whole-buffer
      :v "C-d" #'duplicate-dwim
      :v "C-z" #'undo
      :v "C-y" #'redo
      :n "C-c" #'kill-ring-save
      :n "C-x" #'kill-region
      :n "C-v" #'yank
      :n "C-a" #'mark-whole-buffer
      :n "C-d" #'duplicate-dwim
      :n "C-z" #'undo
      :n "C-y" #'redo
      :i "C-c" #'kill-ring-save
      :i "C-x" #'kill-region
      :i "C-v" #'yank
      :i "C-a" #'mark-whole-buffer
      :i "C-d" #'duplicate-dwim
      :i "C-z" #'undo
      :i "C-y" #'redo)
#+end_src

**  Insert Mode Helpers

These helpers are about flow while typing. They smooth out tiny speed bumps so you stay in the zone. Expect little conveniences that save micro-pauses and keep your cursor moving naturally. The focus is on comfort, rhythm, and staying focused on your text.

#+begin_src emacs-lisp

** Toggle

This gathers simple on/off switches for features you’ll want to flip quickly. It’s about tailoring the environment to the moment—quiet when you need focus, louder when you’re exploring. The design favors instant feedback and reversibility. You can dip into a mode, test the mood, and switch back without ceremony.

#+begin_src emacs-lisp
(map! :leader

      (:prefix ("t" . "open/misc")
       :desc "Toggle minimap"        "m" #'minimap-mode
       :desc "Toggle imenu list"     "i" #'imenu-list-smart-toggle
       :desc "Toggle Treemacs"       "p" #'treemacs
       :desc "Toggle sidebars"       "s" #'my/toggle-sidebars
       :desc "Toggle centaur-tabs"   "t" #'centaur-tabs-mode
       :desc "Switch buffer group"   "g" #'consult-buffer
       :desc "Toggle transparency"   "T" #'my/toggle-transparency
       :desc "(Un)Comment Line"      "-" #'comment-line)
)
#+end_src

* Project/File Management

Here lives the “move between things” toolkit. It orients you within a project, helps you find files, and keeps context close at hand. The idea is to shorten the path from intention to result when hopping between tasks. You get just enough structure to feel organized without getting in your way.

** Project

These are the actions that treat a codebase or notes collection as a single unit. They help you jump to meaningful places, run routine tasks, and keep momentum as you switch workstreams. The emphasis is on quick orientation and seamless transitions. It’s the command center for your current endeavor.

#+begin_src emacs-lisp
(map! :leader
      ;; File/project management)
      :desc "Find project file" "p f" #'projectile-find-file
      :desc "Switch buffer" "b b" #'switch-to-buffer
      :desc "Save buffer" "f s" #'save-buffer)
#+end_src

** Centaur Tabs Navigation

This subsection provides a tabbed way to move among open work. Tabs act as labeled anchors so you can hop between contexts without scrolling through a long list. The approach favors glanceability and quick selection. It’s a familiar, visual system that suits “many things open” workflows.

#+begin_src emacs-lisp
(map!
 :n "M-<right>"     #'centaur-tabs-forward
 :n "M-<left>"      #'centaur-tabs-backward
 :n "C-<right>"     #'centaur-tabs-move-current-tab-to-right
 :n "C-<left>"      #'centaur-tabs-move-current-tab-to-left
 :n "M-S-<right>"   #'centaur-tabs-forward-group
 :n "M-S-<left>"    #'centaur-tabs-backward-group
 :n "M-<up>"        #'centaur-tabs-select-beg-tab
 :n "M-<down>"      #'centaur-tabs-select-end-tab
 :n "M-g"           #'centaur-tabs-switch-group)
#+end_src

* PDF Tools Bindings

Here are the shortcuts for reading and skimming documents. The focus is on navigation, zooming, and light annotation so you can study without friction. It aims to feel like a purpose-built reader that just happens to live inside your editor. You get a smooth loop between reading and note-taking.

#+begin_src emacs-lisp
(map! :map pdf-view-mode-map
      :leader
      :prefix ("o" . "open")
      :desc "PDF outline" "g" #'pdf-outline)

(map! :map pdf-view-mode-map
      :n "g g" #'pdf-view-goto-page)

(general-define-key
 :states 'normal
 :prefix "SPC t p"
 "d" #'pdf-view-midnight-minor-mode
 "m" #'pdf-view-themed-minor-mode
 "w" #'pdf-view-fit-width-to-window
 "p" #'pdf-view-fit-page-to-window)

(map! :map pdf-view-mode-map
      :leader
      :prefix ("o" . "open")
      :desc "Open in Zathura" "z" #'my/open-pdf-in-zathura)
#+end_src


* Consult Zathura Command

This gives you a quick bridge to an external viewer when that’s the better tool for the moment. It’s handy for rapid previews, full-screen reading, or system-level features outside the editor. The intent is convenience rather than lock-in. You can jump out, check something, and come right back.

#+begin_src emacs-lisp
(general-define-key
 :states 'normal
 :prefix "SPC f v"
 "z" #'my/consult-pdf-zathura)
#+end_src


* Org Agenda

This section is your control room for tasks and schedules. It brings plans, deadlines, and next steps into one place where you can review and act. The aim is to keep you oriented: what matters now, what’s next, what can wait. It supports regular check-ins so nothing important slips.

#+begin_src emacs-lisp
(map! :leader
      (:prefix ("o a" . "org")
       :desc "Agenda"           "a" #'org-agenda
       :desc "Capture"          "c" #'org-capture
       :desc "Today"            "t" (lambda! () (interactive) (org-agenda nil "t"))
       :desc "Next actions"     "n" (lambda! () (interactive) (org-agenda nil "n"))
       :desc "Deadlines"        "d" (lambda! () (interactive) (org-agenda nil "d"))
       :desc "Waiting"          "w" (lambda! () (interactive) (org-agenda nil "w"))
       :desc "Projects"         "p" (lambda! () (interactive) (org-agenda nil "p"))
       :desc "Weekly review"    "r" (lambda! () (interactive) (org-agenda nil "r"))))

#+end_src
